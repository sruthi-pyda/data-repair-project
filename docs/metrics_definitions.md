# Detection metrics — exact definitions

**Purpose.** This document answers reviewer gap **G1**
(`docs/research_program.md` §9): *"The report describes the Adult detector as
going from 0.0025 to 0.0120 overall, and to 0.70 on the tampered column. These
numbers are not self-explanatory. Is the metric precision, recall, F1, AUPRC,
or something else?"*

Every number this project reports about **detection** is defined below: the
formula, the population it is computed over, and the failure mode it exists to
guard against. The measured tables that use these definitions are in
`results/w4_metrics.md`, generated by `experiments/w4_metrics.py`.

Implementation: `src/detection_eval/metrics.py` (metrics),
`src/detection_eval/harness.py` (protocol), `src/detection_eval/labels.py`
(labels). Nothing here is a new metric; this document is the specification of
what was already being computed.

---

## 1. The evaluation unit is a CELL

Not a row, not a column, not a repair. The population is every cell of
`dirty.csv`:

| dataset | rows | columns | **cells** | corrupted cells | prevalence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| breast_cancer | 699 | 12 | 8,388 | 631 | 0.075227 |
| smartfactory | 23,645 | 19 | 449,255 | 7,093 | 0.015788 |
| adult | 45,222 | 15 | 678,330 | 1,701 | 0.002508 |

A cell is **positive** (`y = 1`) iff its dirty value differs from the clean
value under `src.evaluation.repair_metrics.ValueComparator` — the same equality
test Phase 10 uses, so detection and repair evaluation cannot disagree about
which cells are broken. `NaN == NaN` and `371 == 371.0` both count as *equal*
(the loader's int64 → float64 promotion is not corruption). Details and the
exact totals: `src/detection_eval/labels.py`.

Clean data enters **only** here, to produce 0/1 labels, and never reaches a
detector (CLAUDE.md §3).

## 2. Notation

For $n$ cells with labels $y_i \in \{0,1\}$ and detector scores
$s_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (higher = more suspicious), a **threshold** $t$ turns
scores into flags:

$$\hat{y}_i(t) = \mathbb{1}[s_i \ge t]$$

$$
\mathrm{TP}(t) = \sum_i \hat y_i y_i \qquad
\mathrm{FP}(t) = \sum_i \hat y_i (1-y_i)
$$
$$
\mathrm{FN}(t) = \sum_i (1-\hat y_i) y_i \qquad
\mathrm{TN}(t) = \sum_i (1-\hat y_i)(1-y_i)
$$

$P = \sum_i y_i$ is the number of positives, $N = n - P$ the number of
negatives.

**Prevalence**

$$\pi = \frac{P}{n}$$

Prevalence is printed beside every score in every table in this project. A
PR-AUC is uninterpretable without it (§4).

**Precision / recall / F1 at a threshold**

$$
\mathrm{Prec}(t) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}(t)}{\mathrm{TP}(t) + \mathrm{FP}(t)}
\qquad
\mathrm{Rec}(t) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}(t)}{\mathrm{TP}(t) + \mathrm{FN}(t)}
= \frac{\mathrm{TP}(t)}{P}
$$

$$
F_1(t) = \frac{2\,\mathrm{Prec}(t)\,\mathrm{Rec}(t)}
{\mathrm{Prec}(t) + \mathrm{Rec}(t)}
= \frac{2\,\mathrm{TP}(t)}{\text{(number flagged)} + P}
$$

Precision's denominator is the number of **flags**, which is why it is the
operationally meaningful axis: of the cells you send to a human, how many were
really broken. Recall's denominator is the number of **corruptions**: of the
errors in the table, how many did you find.

Guards against: a detector that finds everything by flagging everything
(recall 1, precision $\pi$), and a detector that is never wrong because it
flags one cell (precision 1, recall $1/P$). Neither is useful; F1 rejects
both.

## 3. PR-AUC — average precision (**the primary metric**)

Let the distinct score values be swept as thresholds in decreasing order,
indexing by $n = 1, 2, \dots$ the resulting operating points, with
$\mathrm{R}_0 = 0$. Then

$$\boxed{\; \mathrm{AP} = \sum_{n} \left( \mathrm{R}_n - \mathrm{R}_{n-1} \right) \mathrm{P}_n \;}$$

where $\mathrm{P}_n, \mathrm{R}_n$ are the precision and recall at the $n$-th
threshold. This is a right-hand Riemann sum of precision against recall — the
area under the precision–recall curve **without interpolation**. Interpolated
AUPRC (trapezoidal, or the "maximum precision to the right" convention) is
optimistically biased; we do not use it.

- **Computed over:** all cells in the evaluated population, in one ranking.
- **Implementation:** `src.detection_eval.metrics.pr_auc`, wrapping
  `sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score`, which implements exactly the
  formula above.
- **Independently re-derived twice.** `tests/test_detection_eval.py`
  re-implements the formula from the definition and asserts agreement on
  random and adversarial inputs (all-tied scores, two-value scores,
  duplicated extremes). `experiments/w4_metrics.py` additionally re-derives
  $\mathrm{AP} = \sum_n (\mathrm{R}_n - \mathrm{R}_{n-1})\mathrm{P}_n$ from
  the plotted PR curve for every dataset × detector and asserts it matches, so
  the published curve and the published scalar are provably the same object.

### A random or constant scorer achieves PR-AUC = prevalence

If scores carry no information, then at every threshold the flagged set is a
uniformly random subset of the cells, so
$\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Prec}(t)] = \pi$ for all $t$, and

$$\mathrm{AP} = \sum_n (\mathrm{R}_n - \mathrm{R}_{n-1})\,\pi = \pi \sum_n (\mathrm{R}_n - \mathrm{R}_{n-1}) = \pi \cdot 1 = \pi .$$

A **constant** scorer is the degenerate case: one tie group containing every
cell, one operating point at $(\mathrm{R}, \mathrm{P}) = (1, \pi)$, so
$\mathrm{AP} = \pi$ exactly.

**This is why adult's 0.0025 is the floor and not a score.** Adult's
prevalence is $1701/678330 = 0.002508$, and the rule detector's measured
PR-AUC is 0.0025 to four decimals, with a 95 % interval of
[0.0025, 0.0025] (`results/detection_leaderboard.md`). The rule detector is
*indistinguishable from a coin flip* on adult. Its ROC-AUC of 0.4957 is below
0.5, i.e. very slightly anti-correlated. Quoting "0.0025" as a performance
number is a category error: it is the no-skill baseline, drawn as a horizontal
line on every PR curve in `results/w4_metrics.md`.

Because the baseline moves with the dataset, PR-AUC is **never comparable
across datasets** and must always be read against $\pi$, or normalised:

$$\text{lift over chance} = \frac{\mathrm{AP}}{\pi}$$

0.0120 on adult is 4.8× chance; 0.67 on breast_cancer ($\pi = 0.0752$) is 8.9×
chance. The raw numbers suggest the opposite ordering, which is exactly the
confusion G1 is about.

### What PR-AUC measures that a rule detector does not attempt

PR-AUC scores a **ranking**. A rule detector emits a **set**: six fixed
constants (1.00 missing, 0.90 out-of-range, 0.85 format error, 0.70 binary
violation, 0.35 novel category, 0.30 whitespace) plus 0.0 for every unflagged
cell. On adult that is ~672,000 cells sharing one score. Inside a tie group
the ranking carries no information, so the curve has at most as many points as
there are distinct scores. This is a reported structural finding, not a
defect of the measurement — and it is the headroom a probabilistic detector
exploits (`src/detection_eval/harness.py`, `RuleDetectorAdapter`). Every table
in `results/w4_metrics.md` prints the number of distinct scores for this
reason.

## 4. ROC-AUC — reported for comparability, never as evidence

$$\mathrm{ROC\text{-}AUC} = \int_0^1 \mathrm{TPR}\, d(\mathrm{FPR}),
\qquad
\mathrm{TPR} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}}{P},
\quad
\mathrm{FPR} = \frac{\mathrm{FP}}{N}$$

Equivalently, the probability that a uniformly random positive outranks a
uniformly random negative (ties counted as $\tfrac12$).

- **Random scorer:** 0.5, independently of prevalence.
- **Implementation:** `src.detection_eval.metrics.roc_auc`.

**Why it is misleading at 0.25 % prevalence.** Its x-axis denominator is
$N = 676{,}629$. Raising 6,000 false alarms moves FPR by 0.0089 — under one
percentage point. ROC-AUC therefore cannot see the thing that decides whether
a detector is usable: the *share of flags* that are wrong.

Worked example on our own measured numbers (`results/w2_ensemble.md`, adult):
the conditional model alone scores **ROC-AUC 0.8789** — which reads as a good
detector — while on the same scores and the same cells
**precision@1701 = 0.0000**: of the 1,701 most suspicious cells, *not one* is
corrupted. Its PR-AUC is 0.0110. A reader shown only the ROC-AUC would
conclude the detector works. It does not, in the sense that matters.

We print ROC-AUC anyway, flagged, because published detection systems quote it
and leaving it out would make comparison impossible.

## 5. best-F1 — an ORACLE upper bound, not an operating point

$$F_1^{\star} = \max_{t} F_1(t), \qquad t^{\star} = \arg\max_{t} F_1(t)$$

- **Candidate thresholds:** the distinct score values. $\hat y(t)$ can only
  change as $t$ crosses one of them, so sweeping them is exhaustive, not a
  sample.
- **Ties:** resolved to the **highest** threshold attaining the maximum (the
  most conservative detector that gets there).
- **Implementation:** `src.detection_eval.metrics.best_f1`, which returns
  both $F_1^{\star}$ and $t^{\star}$.

**$t^{\star}$ is chosen with knowledge of the labels.** best-F1 is therefore
an upper bound on what a deployed detector could reach, not a measurement of
one. It is comparable across methods only because every method is granted the
same advantage. It must never be presented as operating-point performance, and
`results/w4_metrics.md` prints an explicit **operating-points table**
(threshold, precision, recall, F1, cells flagged) beside it so a reader sees
the trade-off curve rather than one oracle number.

The same caveat applies to the **0.70 on the tampered column** figure the
reviewer asks about: it is `best-F1`-adjacent oracle territory twice over —
PR-AUC 0.6997 measured on `income` *alone*, which requires knowing in advance
that adult's corruption is confined to `income`. That is ground-truth
knowledge. `docs/research_program.md` §8 states the only defensible claim: the
conditional model ranks corruption well *within* a column and cannot tell
which column is worth looking at.

## 6. precision@k, recall@k, lift@k — the budgeted-review view

Let $S_k$ be the top-$k$ cells by score. Then

$$
\mathrm{Prec@}k = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i \in S_k} y_i
\qquad
\mathrm{Rec@}k = \frac{1}{P}\sum_{i \in S_k} y_i
\qquad
\mathrm{Lift@}k = \frac{\mathrm{Prec@}k}{\pi}
$$

- **Default $k = P$** (the number of positives). At that $k$,
  $\mathrm{Prec@}k = \mathrm{Rec@}k$ — the R-precision — which makes it the
  single most comparable operating point across methods on one dataset.
- **Interpretation:** "we have budget to review $k$ cells; what share of that
  budget is well spent, and what share of the errors does it catch?"
- **Ties.** If the $k$-th cell lands inside a group of equal scores, the answer
  depends on how the tie is broken. Breaking by array order would be
  catastrophic here, because cells are enumerated column-major, so index order
  correlates with the column and hence with the label. These functions return
  the **expectation under uniformly random tie-breaking**: whole tie groups
  above the cut contribute all their positives, and the straddling group
  contributes its positives pro rata. That is order-independent, deterministic
  and exactly the value shuffling would converge to.
  (`src.detection_eval.metrics.precision_at_k`.)
- **Lift** is the scale-free companion: precision@k of 0.08 means nothing until
  you know whether $\pi$ is 0.0025 (lift 32, strong) or 0.075 (lift 1.07, no
  signal).

## 7. Accuracy — why it never appears as a headline

$$\mathrm{Acc}(t) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}(t) + \mathrm{TN}(t)}{n} $$

**Worked example, adult, exact.** A detector that flags **nothing** has
$\mathrm{TP} = \mathrm{FP} = 0$ and $\mathrm{TN} = 676{,}629$:

$$\mathrm{Acc} = \frac{676{,}629}{678{,}330} = 0.997492\dots = \mathbf{99.749\,\%}$$

while finding **0 of 1,701** corruptions. Its precision is undefined (no
flags), its recall is 0, its F1 is 0, its PR-AUC is $\pi = 0.0025$ and its
ROC-AUC is 0.5. Six metrics correctly call it worthless; accuracy calls it
99.7 % right.

In general the flag-nothing accuracy is exactly $1 - \pi$: 99.749 % on adult,
98.421 % on smartfactory, 92.477 % on breast_cancer. `results/w4_metrics.md`
prints accuracy for every detector **next to that constant**, purely to
demonstrate that the column carries no information. It is reported to be
dismissed, not to be compared.

## 8. Measurement protocol

Fixed by `src.detection_eval.harness.evaluate_detector` so that no route can
tune it:

- **5 splits**, seed **42**, each holding a stratified **30 %** of the cells
  for evaluation. Split $i$ uses `default_rng(42 + i)`, so the set is
  reproducible from the seed alone and adding a split never changes the
  earlier ones.
- **Stratified on the label**: each eval split contains 30 % of the positives
  *and* 30 % of the negatives. Without this, an unstratified 30 % split of
  adult would hold $510 \pm 22$ positives by chance and per-split PR-AUC would
  partly measure the dice. Stratifying fixes per-split prevalence to the
  population value, leaving only *which* positives vary — the noise the
  interval is meant to describe.
- **Confidence intervals**: percentile bootstrap over the 5 per-split values,
  via `src.experiments.aggregate.bootstrap_interval` (95 %, seeded). With
  $n = 5$ the interval is wide, and honest about being wide. It measures
  **split variance only**: for a detector with no `fit` it is pure
  evaluation-sample noise, not model variance.
- **Leakage**: labels are never passed to a detector — `fit(dirty_df,
  train_mask)` has nowhere to put them — and a detector that fits is refitted
  per split on train cells only. A detector with no `fit` is scored once and
  that scoring is reused, because there is nothing to leak.
- **Full-dataset tables.** The per-column, false-positive and operating-point
  tables in `results/w4_metrics.md` are computed on **all** cells at the
  full-set oracle threshold, not on one split, so that every corruption in the
  dataset appears in exactly one row of the accounting and the counts add up
  to the dataset totals in §1. This is legitimate for the four detectors
  measured there because none of them fits on labels and the conditional
  scores are produced **out of fold over rows** (5 folds: every cell's score
  comes from a model that never saw that cell's row), so
  `full_is_in_sample` is `False` for all of them. For any route that *does*
  fit per split, the honest numbers are the per-split ones.

## 9. Undefined renders "—", never 0.0

`0.0` in a results table reads as a measured score. CLAUDE.md §5.4 records
that exact mistake being made once already — a not-fitted calibrator printing
`Brier 0.0000` and looking perfect. Every metric function returns `None` when
the quantity is mathematically undefined, and every table renders `None` as
`—`:

| situation | undefined |
|---|---|
| no positives in the population | PR-AUC, recall@k, best-F1, lift@k |
| single class (all 0 or all 1) | ROC-AUC |
| $k = 0$ (nothing flagged) | precision@k |
| nothing flagged at threshold $t$ | precision($t$) |
| prevalence 0 | lift@k |

Legitimately `0.0`, and printed as such: F1 at a threshold where positives
exist but none is flagged. That is a measured failure, not a missing value.

## 10. Reading the three numbers in the reviewer's question

| quoted | what it actually is | protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **0.0025** | PR-AUC (average precision) of the **rule** detector on **all 678,330 adult cells**. Equals prevalence to four decimals ⇒ no information. | 5 stratified cell splits, seed 42; `results/detection_leaderboard.md` |
| **0.0120** | PR-AUC of the **conditional** detector on all adult cells — 4.8× chance. Measured under the wave-1 **row-split** protocol of `experiments/r2_conditional.py` (70/30 rows, stratified on "row has a corrupted cell"). | `results/r2_conditional.md`; the cell-split harness gives 0.0110 for the same detector (`results/w2_ensemble.md`, `cond_only`) — `results/w4_metrics.md` measures it again under the one protocol, with both numbers side by side |
| **0.70** | PR-AUC of the conditional detector restricted to the **`income` column only** (0.6997). Partly oracle: it presumes knowledge that corruption is confined to `income`. | `results/r2_conditional.md` §per-column; see §5 above |

All three are the same metric — average precision, §3 — on three different
cell populations. The populations, not the metric, explain the spread. That is
the answer to G1, and `results/w4_metrics.md` makes it checkable per column.
