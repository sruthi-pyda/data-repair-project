# W5.1a — complete costs: what undetected corruption actually costs

Route W5.1a of `docs/research_program.md` §11, reviewer **gap 4a**.
Generated by `experiments/w5_complete_costs.py`. Seed 42, full datasets,
mock repair mode, shipped detector defaults, costs from
`configs/costs.yaml` (abstract experimental penalty units — never money).

## 0. What changed and what did not

`CostEvaluator.evaluate(..., include_false_negatives=True)` adds

```
surcharge = C_abstain x |{genuinely corrupted cells} - {flagged cells}|
```

`C_abstain` (`c_leave_error`) is corruption-persistence cost
(`docs/cost_semantics.md` §1). An undetected corruption is an unrepaired
error; the only difference from an abstention is that nobody noticed, and
the data does not care. So no new cost parameter is invented.

The mode defaults to **off**, so every previously published number is
unchanged bit-for-bit (asserted in `tests/test_complete_costs.py`, and
re-verified against `results/RESULTS.md` §1 in §4 below).

Detection recall is the **ceiling on what any decision policy can
address**, however well calibrated. The surcharge is the part of total
harm that no policy in this project touches.

- **adult**: 1,701 corrupted cells (docs/cost_semantics.md §3 quotes 1,701; agrees).

## 3. Does the policy ranking change? **No — and it cannot.**

The surcharge depends on the detector's flag *set* and on ground truth,
not on which action the policy chose. For a fixed detector it therefore
adds the same constant to every policy, which cannot reorder them. This
is proved exhaustively over the action space in
`tests/test_complete_costs.py::TestSurchargeIsPolicyInvariant` and
re-measured per dataset here:

| dataset | surcharge identical across all policies? | ranking identical? | top policy (both modes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| adult | yes (8,505) | yes | `always-abstain`, `proposed (calibrated risk-aware)` — **tied** at 8,505 |
| breast_cancer | yes (1,690) | yes | `proposed (calibrated risk-aware)` |
| smartfactory | yes (0) | yes | `always-human`, `conf>=0.70`, `conf>=0.80`, `conf>=0.90`, `proposed (calibrated risk-aware)` — **tied** at 14,186 |

So the honest statement is **not** "pricing false negatives changes which
policy wins". It is:

> The zero-regret ranking survives intact, because the omission flattered
> every policy by exactly the same amount. What does not survive is the
> *magnitude* of the claim.

- **adult**: the proposed policy's cost goes 0 → **8,505**. Against the only baseline that prices the whole problem (do-nothing, 8,505) that is a **0.0% reduction in total harm**.
- **breast_cancer**: the proposed policy's cost goes 1,615 → **3,305**. Against the only baseline that prices the whole problem (do-nothing, 5,240) that is a **36.9% reduction in total harm**.
- **smartfactory**: the proposed policy's cost goes 14,186 → **14,186**. Against the only baseline that prices the whole problem (do-nothing, 35,465) that is a **60.0% reduction in total harm**.

### 4b. A derived identity the two modes must satisfy

`always-abstain` leaves every *flagged* corruption in place, and the
surcharge leaves every *unflagged* one in place. Its total harm must
therefore equal `n_corrupted x C_abstain` exactly — which is also the
do-nothing cost. Nothing in the implementation enforces this; it is an
independent re-derivation of the same quantity by two routes.

| dataset | always-abstain total | n_corrupted x C_abstain | do-nothing | identical? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| adult | 8,505 | 8,505 | 8,505 | yes |
| breast_cancer | 5,240 | 5,240 | 5,240 | yes |
| smartfactory | 35,465 | 35,465 | 35,465 | yes |

**Identity holds on every dataset: True.**

## 5. What the paper must now say

1. **Adult's "0" is 8,505.** The flagged-only
   cost is genuinely 0, and that result stands: all 5,829 flags are false alarms (0 of them genuinely corrupted), and the
   policy correctly declines to act on every one of them.
   But 1,701 genuine corruptions were never
   detected, so total harm is 1,701 x 5 = **8,505**, which is
   *exactly* the do-nothing cost. On Adult the pipeline reduces total
   data-quality harm by **0%**. The flagged-only win is a win about
   not wasting review effort on false alarms, and nothing more.
2. **Both numbers must always appear together.** Flagged-only answers
   "was the decision right?"; total harm answers "is the data better?".
   Reporting only the first is what gap 4a objects to.
3. **Detection recall is the ceiling**, and it is the quantity the
   research program's wave 1–3 work attacks. The decision layer cannot
   improve it by any amount of calibration.
4. **The ranking result is unaffected**, and saying so is stronger than
   hiding the surcharge: the omission was uniform, not selective.
