# W5.2b — does the Raha comparison survive dirty profiling?

`results/w5_raha_comparison.md` beats Raha on three of four datasets
using **zero labels** against Raha's twenty, but recorded that the
comparison was **not settled**: our rule detector's fences, ranges and
vocabularies come from `DatasetProfile`, built from `clean_df`, so
"zero labels" is not "no clean knowledge".

W5.1b settled that for breast_cancer and found the caveat **material**
(PR-AUC -0.1488, disjoint CIs). It did not cover hospital, rayyan or
beers — three quarters of the head-to-head. This closes that gap by
recomputing our natural operating point with **every profile statistic
derived from dirty data**. The dirty arm uses no clean data of any
kind, so it is the number that can face Raha's without an asterisk.

Generated by `experiments/w5_raha_leakage_check.py`. Seed 42, full
datasets, `score > 0` operating point (a rule detector is a *set*, so
no threshold is tuned and no oracle is used).

## What this settles, and what it does not

Settled: whether our wins over Raha depend on clean-derived profile
statistics, on all four datasets rather than one.

Not settled: Raha still ran at a single 20-label budget and one seed
per dataset, and the four larger datasets exhausted memory with
OpenBLAS allocation failures. Raha's breast_cancer result remains
anomalous (13 flags out of 8,388, recall 0.021) and is still not
quoted as evidence of anything. And this compares **detection only** —
Raha makes no repair/review/abstain decision at all, which is where
this project's actual contribution lies.
