# W6.1 — M2: per-column contamination fraction as the triage signal

Generated by `experiments/w6_contamination.py`. Planned in
`docs/w6_plan.md` §4, committed before the code. Seed 42.

M2 was ranked **#1 by value/effort** in
`docs/column_triage_research.md` before any of the four candidate
mechanisms was tested. Two of the other three are now refuted: M1 the
likelihood ratio (`experiments/w4_ratio_test.py`) and self-supervised
injection (W5.3, `results/w5_column_triage_control.md`).

## 0. The hypothesis

A rare-but-valid value and a corrupted value are *both* improbable, so
no monotone function of `P(x)` can separate them. What differs is
**separability**:

- a uniformly-rare-but-**clean** column — its rare values are the tail
  of *one* component, so the anomalous mixture weight is **low**
- a **corrupted** column — genuine two-component structure, so the
  weight is **high**

`pi_hat_c` is also a **proportion**: dimensionless, hence comparable
across columns by construction, which `docs/w6_plan.md` §2 argues is
exactly what per-cell score aggregation lacks.

## 1. Estimator self-check on known answers (read this first)

Each estimator is run on score distributions whose contamination is
known by construction. **An estimator that cannot keep
`clean_skewed` low cannot rank a clean heavy-tailed column below a
corrupted one, which is the whole task.**

| case | true | mixmod | gmm | dbic | what it tests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `clean_skewed` | 0.0000 | 0.1360 | 0.3107 | 1793.5 | one skewed component, NO contamination — a clean heavy-tailed column. Must come out LOW. |
| `clean_uniformish` | 0.0000 | 0.0702 | 0.3441 | 2246.2 | one tight component, no contamination. Must come out LOW. |
| `contaminated_04` | 0.0400 | 0.0424 | 0.0402 | 10615.1 | genuine two-component mixture at 4 %, the adult `income` rate. |
| `contaminated_20` | 0.2000 | 0.2014 | 0.2040 | 12109.1 | genuine two-component mixture at 20 %. |

`dbic` is a BIC *difference*, not a fraction — compare it across rows,
never against the `true` column.

## 2. Headline — column-level AUC, pooled

Does the statistic separate corrupted columns from clean ones?
Chance is 0.5. Reported **only** on datasets where both classes are
present; `results/w5_column_sparsity.md` records why adult alone
cannot settle this (power ceiling p = 1/15 = 0.067).

**Columns the detector skips are excluded.** An ID-like,
high-cardinality-text or constant column gets an all-zero score
vector, which carries no information about contamination. Leaving
those in makes the ranking measure *whether the detector scored the
column at all* — on hospital that is 12 of 20 columns, and it is why
an earlier version of this table had mixmod matching the mean-score
baseline to four decimals on six datasets. The baseline below is

## 4. The pre-registered falsification test

`docs/w6_plan.md` §4 fixed this in advance:

> On adult: `pi_hat(income) > pi_hat(native_country)`?
> If not, the mechanism is dead.

`income` is 3.8 % corrupted; `native_country` is **0 %** corrupted and
is the column that currently dominates the top of our ranking.

| estimator | pi_hat(income) | pi_hat(native_country) | income > native_country? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| mixmod (primary) | 0.3662 | 0.2016 | **PASS** |
| gmm | 0.4284 | 0.2016 | **PASS** |
| dbic | 4745.1416 | 59892.7996 | **REFUTED** |

On the primary estimator (`mixmod`) the mechanism
**survives** this test. That is one comparison on one
dataset, so read §2 and §3 before believing it.

## 6. Leakage

`pi_hat_c` comes from dirty-table scores only. The detector is fitted
with `profile=None`, so it is fully self-supervised and no clean data
of any kind — not even aggregate statistics — enters the signal. Clean
data is read only to score the ranking after it exists, which is the
permitted evaluation use (`CLAUDE.md` §3). Note this makes W6.1
**zero-knowledge**, so unlike the shipped detector it is not exposed
to the soft-leakage result in `results/w5_leakage_ablation.md`.
