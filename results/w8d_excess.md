# W8-D — Excess Surprise: does a calibration gap localise corruption?

Generated by `experiments/w8d_excess.py`. Mechanism and motivation in
`docs/w8_plan.md` §8-§9. Seed 42.

## 0. What was pre-registered, before this ran

From the unit tests in `tests/test_excess_surprise.py`:

- **Difficulty cancellation is VERIFIED** on synthetic data. Clean-easy
  and clean-hard columns whose observed errors differ by more than 0.3
  both land within 0.15 of zero excess, with an excess gap under a
  third of their observed gap. No earlier mechanism here removed that
  nuisance parameter.
- **Sensitivity is WEAK**: excess rises at about `0.08 * r`, because the
  model is cross-fitted on the corrupted frame and absorbs some
  corruption into `expected_error`. At adult's true `income` rate of
  ~0.0376 that predicts excess near **0.003**.

So the prediction on record: **if this fails on adult, weak sensitivity
is the reason, not failed cancellation.**

## 4. Condition 3 — cell level (a COMBINATION, not a standalone detector)

Excess Surprise is a per-column statistic, so it cannot rank cells by
itself. Here `excess_c` is used as a per-column prior multiplying the
existing per-cell score, over scored columns only. Reference points:
adult whole-table PR-AUC **0.0025** is chance, and the within-`income`
real-corruption Bayes ceiling is **0.7222** with the existing detector
