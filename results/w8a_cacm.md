# W8-A — CACM, the contamination-aware conditional mixture

Generated by `experiments/w8a_cacm.py`. Mechanism: `src/detectors/cacm.py`. Pre-registered falsification conditions: `docs/w8_plan.md` §5. Every number below came from this run; nothing is carried over from an earlier wave except the three reference figures explicitly labelled as quotations.

Configuration: seed 42, row cap 8000, `n_bins=10`, `em_iters=10`, `n_folds=2`, `r_init=0.3`, `g` initialised to the column marginal, additive smoothing 1.0, `r` clamped to [1e-4, 0.5]. `f` is `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (`max_iter=30`) refitted each M-step with `sample_weight = 1 - z`, and every `f(v|x)` used in an E-step is an **out-of-fold** prediction.

## The mechanism

```
P_obs(v | x) = (1 - r_c) * f(v | x)   <- clean:      DEPENDS on x
             +      r_c  * g(v)       <- corruption: does NOT

P(corrupt | v, x) = r_c*g(v) / [ (1-r_c)*f(v|x) + r_c*g(v) ]
```

`x`-independence of `g` is the identifying restriction. Closest prior art is Kubica & Moore, *Probabilistic Noise Identification and Data Cleaning* (CMU 2002/2003); see `docs/w8_plan.md` §3 for what is and is not new here.

## Condition 1 & 5 — column triage, on columns the detector can score

`CACM post` is the mean posterior; `CACM r` is the fitted contamination rate; `baseline` is the mean raw `ConditionalDetector(mode="full")` score, recomputed on the same columns in the same run.

## Conditions 4 & 6 — is it just rarity, or just cardinality?

Spearman rho between the per-column statistic and two confounds: the rare-value fraction (share of cells whose value occurs in under 1 % of rows) and the number of distinct values. A high correlation means the statistic is a proxy for the confound, which is how W5.3's self-supervised injection and the marginal-rarity mechanisms died. ## Condition 7 — leakage

Both detectors are fitted with `profile=None` on the dirty frame alone; `cell_labels(clean, dirty)` is not called until both score frames exist, and this script asserts that ordering. `src/detectors/cacm.py` ignores `profile` entirely, so passing a clean-derived `DatasetProfile` cannot inherit the soft-leakage caveat of CLAUDE.md §3. `tests/test_cacm.py::TestNoLeakage` asserts it mechanically: a signature scan of every entry point for any parameter mentioning clean/label/truth, a profile object that raises on any attribute read, and a spy on `HistGradientBoostingClassifier.fit` checking that every value reaching it is a value of the dirty frame.
