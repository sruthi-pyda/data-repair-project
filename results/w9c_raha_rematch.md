## How the comparison is made fair

Raha emits a flag **set** at a 20-label budget, so its F1 is one
operating point. Ours emits a continuous score, where the threshold
decides the F1 entirely. Three points, and **only the middle one is
like-for-like**:

- **zero-label** — flag every cell any detector scores above zero. No
  labels at all, so a *harder* setting than Raha's.
- **20-label** — Raha's own budget and protocol: sample 20 cells, reveal them, pick the best threshold. **The fair
  comparison.**
- **oracle best-F1** — threshold chosen knowing every label. An
  **upper bound, never an operating point.**

Every arm is zero clean-knowledge: the conditional detector is fitted
with `profile=None` and the rule detector's profile is built from the
dirty frame in both positions.

## Caveats that still stand

- Raha ran at **one** budget and **one** seed per dataset, and the four
  larger datasets exhausted memory with OpenBLAS allocation failures.
- Raha's `breast_cancer` result remains anomalous — 13 flags out of
  8,388, recall 0.021 — far below its published behaviour, and it is
  still not quoted as evidence of anything.
- This compares **detection only**. Raha makes no repair, review or
  abstain decision, which is where this project's contribution lies.
