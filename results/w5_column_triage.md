# W5.3 — column triage by self-supervised injection

Route W5.3, reviewer **gap 2**. Generated by
`experiments/w5_column_triage.py`. Pre-registered in
`docs/w5_3_column_triage_plan.md`, committed **before** this
experiment was written. Seed 42.

## 0. The question

Our detector finds the odd cell *inside* a column but has no idea
*which* column to look at: on adult, **zero** of the 5,000
highest-scored cells are in the tampered `income` column.

The probe plants corruption in one column at a time, refits, and
measures how well it recovers what it planted:

    d_c = ROC-AUC(planted vs non-planted cells of column c)
    t_c = -d_c

**H1**: an already-corrupted column has its model fitted on polluted
targets, so planted corruption is *harder* to find there — lower
`d_c`. The detector is fitted with `profile=None`, so **no clean data
of any kind enters the probe**.

## 1. Setup

| dataset | rows used | of | columns | corrupted columns |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| adult | 4,000 | 45,222 | 15 | 1: `income` |

Rows are subsampled (seeded) to 4,000 where larger. Row sampling
preserves each column's corruption **fraction**, which is the
quantity H1 depends on; detector hyperparameters are shipped
defaults. A full-adult probe is 136 s to fit plus 52 s to score, so
15 columns x 4 types would be ~3 hours for one dataset.

Plant fraction: 5% of the target column's cells.
A column the injector cannot touch for a given type is reported
**ineligible** and excluded, rather than given an invented score.

## 3. Reading the ranking

`t_c = -d_c`, so rank 1 is the column where planted corruption was
**hardest** to recover — the most suspicious column under H1. A
probe type whose `d_c` is ~1.0 for every column carries no column
signal at all: the planted corruption is trivially detectable
everywhere, so the statistic cannot discriminate. That is a real
limit of the method and is visible directly in the tables above.
