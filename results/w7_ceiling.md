# W7.1 — the Bayes-optimal ceiling for corruption detection

> **SUPERSEDED FOR THE REAL DATA — see `results/w7_real_ceiling.md`.**
> The 0.2723 ceiling and the "6.4x available" reading below are for
> **frequency-weighted `swap_valid`** corruption. Inspecting the shipped data
> shows adult's real corruption is nothing of the kind: all 1,701 flips are
> `1 -> 0`, entirely one-directional, majority class to minority class. For
> the corruption actually present, the within-column ceiling is **0.7222**
> and our detector reaches **0.5626** — we already capture **77.9 %**.
> So **do not quote 6.4x for real adult**; the real within-column headroom is
> about 1.28x, and the loss is cross-column instead.

Generated by `experiments/w7_ceiling.py`. Planned in

Corruption is **injected by us into a clean table**, so the rate `r`,
the injection distribution `P_inject` and the exact positions are known
by construction. That makes the optimal posterior computable:

    P(corrupt | v,x) = r*P_inject(v) / [ r*P_inject(v) + (1-r)*P_clean(v|x) ]

Ranking by it gives the **PR-AUC no method could exceed**. The headline
is then `captured = PR-AUC(ours) / PR-AUC(ceiling)`.

exactly 1 on injected cells and 0 elsewhere. **The ceiling must come

**12 condition(s) have a VOID control** and are excluded
from the summary below: `breast_cancer`. The
two columns there differ by less than 0.02 in
rare-value fraction, so the comparison measures column-specific
score *scale*, not rarity. On breast_cancer both candidates have
rare_frac 0.000 and the result is driven by `Clump Thickness`
carrying a mean score near 0.43 against `class` near 0.01 —
nothing to do with rare-but-valid values. breast_cancer has no
heavy-tailed *scored* column and cannot host this control.

On the hard case — config A, `swap_valid`, a corrupted low-tail
column against a clean heavy-tailed one, **valid controls only** —
the mean-score statistic ranks the corrupted column first in
**3 of 3** conditions.

**All of them.** This *contradicts* the prediction pre-registered
in `docs/w7_plan.md` §3.5(3), which said every statistic tested
so far would rank the clean heavy-tailed column first. On a
cleanly-injected table it does not. The original adult failure
is therefore **not** a simple rarity artefact — something about
the real dirty frame, not rarity alone, produces it.

Config **A** corrupts the low-tail column and leaves the heavy-tailed
one clean — the hard, real case. Config **B** is the reverse.

**Oracle degenerate in 6 condition(s)** — it fell back
to the marginal, so the ceiling there reflects marginal rarity
only and understates what a conditional oracle could do.

Base frames are the **clean** tables, so every positive is one we
injected and no pre-existing corruption confounds the measurement.
